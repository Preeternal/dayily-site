Privacy Policy

This policy explains what the Dayily mobile app currently stores, reads, sends, and writes. It is written for the current implementation and may be updated as the app changes.

Last updated: 2 October 2026

Data we handleHealth dataAccounts and cloud syncDiagnosticsSharingYour choices

1. Data we handle

Dayily is designed to work locally first. Depending on the features you use, the app may handle:

  • workout sessions, exercises, sets, repetitions, load type and load, timing, titles, notes, templates, favorites, and a body-weight snapshot associated with a workout;
  • local profile and preference data, such as language, units, workout settings, and whether health integration is enabled;
  • account data from Firebase Authentication, such as an authentication identifier, email address, verification state, and limited profile information supplied by Google, Apple, or email sign-in. When Apple provides a name or private-relay email, the app may retain the corresponding profile fields;
  • health and activity records made available by your device after you grant permission;
  • technical information needed to operate authentication, cloud sync, crash reporting, and in-app feedback.

Dayily does not store passwords, Firebase refresh tokens, Apple identity tokens, or Apple authorization codes in its own local preferences.

2. Health and activity data

With your permission, Dayily reads data from Apple Health on iOS or Health Connect on Android. The current code can request access to categories such as workouts, active energy, distance, heart rate, heart-rate variability, steps, sleep, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, and body weight. Apple devices may also provide electrocardiogram summaries and wrist temperature when the device supports them. The exact data depends on your device, platform, permissions, and what your watch or band records.

Dayily uses these records on the device to build reports, show activities from other apps or devices, and look for a time-matched activity that can be shown with a Dayily workout. Health records are not used for advertising or marketing.

Dayily can also request separate permission to write a Dayily workout to Apple Health or Health Connect. That write is optional and is controlled by the platform permission you grant. Records already held by Apple Health, Health Connect, another app, or your device are managed by those services; Dayily does not claim ownership of or universal deletion control over them.

At present, health measurements read from Apple Health or Health Connect are used for local reports. The cloud sync contract is for Dayily workout and related journal data; it is not a general upload of your health database.

3. Accounts and cloud sync

You can use Dayily without an account. If you sign in and leave cloud sync enabled, the app uses Firebase Authentication for sign-in and sends eligible Dayily account and workout data to Dayily’s Cloudflare Worker and D1 database so it can synchronize your journal across devices.

Cloud workout data can include completed or cancelled workout sessions, exercise and set details, timestamps, notes, templates, favorites, and the body-weight snapshot stored with a workout. The server uses a stable internal account identifier to keep one user’s data separate from another’s. The app sends the Firebase ID token for authentication; it does not send database credentials.

Local history remains on your device and is not made dependent on network access. The product’s Free and Premium cloud retention rules may limit what is retained in the cloud, but they do not charge you for keeping your local workout history. Any future retention limits will not be treated as a command to delete the local copy.

Deleting a Dayily account currently requests deletion of the Dayily cloud account data, then deletes the Firebase account and the local profile and its workouts. You may need to re-authenticate first. Data held independently by Apple Health, Health Connect, Firebase, the app stores, or other services may require a separate action in those services.

4. Diagnostics and support

Release builds use Sentry for crash reports, technical diagnostics, and the in-app feedback flow. The current configuration enables default PII collection for Sentry and may associate diagnostics with an authenticated email address or provider identifier so a support issue can be investigated. Sentry may also receive device, app, network, and error context needed for diagnostics.

Product analytics uses Firebase Analytics for limited navigation and workout lifecycle events. These events are designed not to include workout or health payloads, search strings, passwords, tokens, or user identifiers. Sentry logs and issues are likewise designed to exclude workout and health payloads from their diagnostic fields.

In-app feedback is user initiated. If you send it, your message and any name or email you enter are sent to the feedback provider. A screenshot is captured and sent only when you choose that action. Sentry Session Replay is configured for release builds; its collection and masking are handled by the Sentry SDK.

Dayily does not use workout or health data for advertising or marketing. The public website does not add analytics, advertising SDKs, cookies, or tracking scripts.

5. Sharing and third parties

Dayily can create a local image of a workout or progress card and open the operating system’s share sheet when you choose to share it. The receiving app or service then handles the copy you send.

The app relies on these service providers for the current functionality: Firebase Authentication and Analytics, Cloudflare Worker/D1 for authenticated cloud sync, Apple Health or Google Health Connect for device health data, and Sentry for diagnostics and feedback. Their own terms and privacy policies also apply to their processing.

Dayily does not sell health or workout data and does not disclose it for advertising or marketing.

6. Your choices

  • You can use the core local journal without signing in.
  • You can grant, refuse, or later change Apple Health or Health Connect permissions in the platform settings.
  • You can turn off cloud sync by signing out; the local history remains on the device.
  • You can request account deletion from the account settings in the app.
  • You can send a privacy question through the public Dayily project contact channel: github.com/Preeternal/dayily/issues.

We will update this page when the data practices materially change. The stable privacy URL is preeternal.github.io/dayily-site/privacy/.
